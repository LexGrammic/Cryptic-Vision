**Project:** Cryptic Vision

# Purpose

The Patient Lifecycle defines the standardized observation, analysis, remediation, verification, and archival process used by the Cryptic Vision Platform.

Within Cryptic Vision, a **Patient (System / Target)** represents any observable engineering asset including software projects, runtimes, applications, repositories, services, infrastructure, or engineering artifacts.

The medical terminology provides an intuitive operational model while the technical terminology maintains compatibility with modern software engineering practices.

Every Patient (System) follows the same lifecycle to ensure repeatable observation, verification, reporting, remediation, and continuous improvement.

# Lifecycle Stages

## 1. Patient Identified (System / Target Identified)

A software system requiring observation is identified.

The engineering target is selected.

Initial metadata is collected.

No modification occurs.

---

## 2. Admitted (Registered for Observation)

The patient (system) enters the observation workflow.

A unique identity is assigned.

Observation records are created.

The patient becomes active within the Med Bay (Observation Environment).

---

## 3. Observed (Environment Inspection)

The operating environment is examined.

Current behavior is documented.

Dependencies are reviewed.

Observation remains passive.

No remediation occurs.

---

## 4. Scanned (System Analysis)

Engineering analysis gathers runtime information.

System structure is evaluated.

Configuration is inspected.

Metrics and telemetry are collected.

Evidence replaces assumptions.

---

## 5. Diagnosed (Assessment)

Collected evidence is analyzed.

Risks are identified.

System health is determined.

Verified findings become the official engineering assessment.

---

## 6. Reported (Engineering Report)

Observation results are documented.

Engineering reports are generated.

Telemetry is summarized.

Recommendations are recorded.

Reports become permanent engineering artifacts.

---

## 7. Treatment Planned (Remediation Plan)

Corrective actions are evaluated.

Repair strategy is selected.

Risk is assessed.

Expansion opportunities are documented.

Treatment must be verified before execution.

---

## 8. Treatment Applied (Remediation Executed)

Approved engineering actions are performed.

Repairs are executed.

Configuration changes are applied.

Only verified remediation procedures are allowed.

---

## 9. Verification (Post-Repair Validation)

The patient (system) is re-examined.

Health improvements are confirmed.

Reports are updated.

Validation must succeed before discharge.

---

## 10. Recovered (System Stabilized)

The patient returns to a stable operational state.

Health metrics improve.

Risks are reduced.

Recovery status becomes part of the engineering history.

---

## 11. Discharged (Observation Complete)

Active observation concludes.

The patient leaves the Med Bay.

Historical records remain available.

Future observation remains possible.

---

## 12. Archived (Historical Record)

Patient history (system history) is preserved.

Reports remain searchable.

Telemetry remains available.

Archived engineering records support future analysis.

# Patient States

Typical patient (system) states include:

• Identified (Target Selected)

• Admitted (Registered)

• Observing (Environment Inspection)

• Scanning (System Analysis)

• Diagnosed (Assessment Complete)

• Reported (Engineering Report Generated)

• Treatment Planned (Remediation Planned)

• Treating (Remediation Active)

• Verified (Validation Passed)

• Recovered (Stable)

• Discharged (Observation Complete)

• Archived (Historical Record)

Patient state remains explicit throughout the lifecycle.

# Engineering Guarantees

The Patient Lifecycle guarantees:

• Observation before modification

• Assessment before remediation

• Verification before discharge

• Explicit patient (system) state

• Repeatable engineering workflow

• Permanent engineering history

• Reproducible reports

• Standardized observation methodology

# Relationship to Pocket Runtime Doctor

Pocket Runtime Doctor serves as the primary observation interface.

It admits patients (systems).

Performs observation and analysis.

Generates engineering assessments.

Produces reports.

Supports verification.

Discharges recovered systems.

---

# Relationship to Med Bay

Med Bay is the Observation Environment.

Every patient (system) progresses through the lifecycle while remaining observable.

Historical engineering records remain available throughout the observation process.

---

# Relationship to Surgery Room

Surgery Room is the Controlled Execution Environment.

Only verified assessments become remediation procedures.

Engineering modifications follow recommendations established during observation.
