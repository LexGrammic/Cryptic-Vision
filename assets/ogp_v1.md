# Occult Grammar Protocol (OGP)

---

# Purpose

The Occult Grammar Protocol (OGP) is the engineering doctrine that governs the design, verification, execution, and evolution of every system within the Cryptic Vision platform.

It provides a repeatable methodology for engineering software through observation-first architecture.

This document serves as the engineering specification for the OGP visual diagram.

---

# Core Law

```text
Environment

↓

Verify

↓

Execute

↓

Expand
```

Every engineering activity follows this sequence.

Expansion is permitted only after verification.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

OGP treats software engineering as an observable process.

Systems are studied before modification, verified before execution, and expanded only after stable behavior has been demonstrated.

The objective is repeatable engineering rather than uncontrolled development.

---

# Engineering Flow

```text
1. Environment
        ↓
2. Verify
        ↓
3. Execute
        ↓
4. Expand
```

## Environment

Responsibilities

- Observe the system
- Understand structure
- Identify components
- Collect state
- Perform no modification

---

## Verify

Responsibilities

- Validate state
- Confirm compatibility
- Assess integrity
- Identify risk
- Establish trusted knowledge

---

## Execute

Responsibilities

- Perform approved actions
- Use verified runtime services
- Produce observable artifacts
- Record outcomes

---

## Expand

Responsibilities

- Introduce new capability
- Preserve verified systems
- Maintain stability
- Document architectural change

---

# Engineering Principles

The protocol is governed by these principles.

- Observe before modification.
- Unknown systems remain under observation until verified.
- Every change leaves an observable artifact.
- State is explicit.
- Presentation remains separate from logic.
- Stable systems are preserved.
- Expand only after verification.

---

# Engineering Rules

1. One step only.
2. Verify before expansion.
3. Do not modify stable systems unless required.
4. Observation first.
5. Repair second.
6. Separate presentation from logic.
7. Use bridge/state files.
8. Avoid duplicate logic.
9. Explicit command triggers only.
10. Clean insertion only.
11. If state is unknown: observe, validate, do not mutate.
